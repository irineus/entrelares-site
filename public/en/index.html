The idea

Two homes. One clear path between them.

The child goes back and forth. What cannot go back and forth is the agreement: whose day each day is, when it changes, and who approved the change. That is what Entrelares keeps — identical for both homes.

What happens today

Why not just use a chat app or a spreadsheet?

Because an agreement buried in a conversation turns into an argument: the message gets lost, each of you remembers it differently, and the child is caught in the middle. The app exists to take the schedule out of "nobody told me".

  • One calendar, identical for both parents
  • Whose day it is today, always in sight
  • A swap asks for the other parent's approval
  • History with date and time that cannot be deleted
  • Instant notifications — nobody is left out

How it works

Three steps. Ten minutes.

Each step with a real screen of the app — no staged demo.

Create the family and invite the other parent

Each of you signs in with your own account. You can also include grandparents and other caregivers who take part in the routine.

Build the calendar together

Set whose day each day is, following your own agreement — alternating weeks, fortnights or a cycle of your own. Holidays and special dates you adjust on the day itself, or on several days at once.

Need to swap a day? Propose it in the app

The other parent approves or declines, and until they answer the day stays as it was. Everything is recorded, with date and time.

Irineu — founder of Entrelares, and a father in shared custody
Read the whole story: why I built Entrelares

I was married for 20 years, and that marriage gave me the most precious thing in my life: my son. When his mother and I realized we would not stay together, we chose to separate with everyone's good in mind — his above all.

Suddenly everything was new: the house, the routine, the habits. But one thing didn't change — wanting to be present in my son's life. Amid the natural friction of a recent separation, what we needed most was exactly what seemed hardest: giving a small child stability and predictability, while fitting everything around his mother's demanding work schedule.

We both needed to know clearly whose day each day was. I tried a spreadsheet — it helped at first, but it soon got complicated and was never at hand when it mattered. I tried an ordinary calendar — too much work to keep up. So I built something of my own, something simple, and it grew until it became a true partner in the time I share with my son.

Talking with other mothers and fathers who had been through a separation, I found the same gap. That's why I decided to open the tool to other families — to take that friction out of the way at such a delicate moment, one you get through with care, love and, above all, respect between everyone involved.

From the browser (Chrome):

  1. Go to web.entrelares.app in Chrome.
  2. Tap Install when the prompt appears — or open the ⋮ menu and choose Install app.
  3. Done: the icon shows up on your home screen, like any other app.

iPhone and iPad (Safari)

  1. Go to web.entrelares.app in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share button (the square with an arrow pointing up). If you can’t see it, tap the Page Menu button and choose Share.
  3. Scroll the list and tap Add to Home Screen. If it isn’t there, tap Edit Actions at the bottom of the list and add it.
  4. Turn on Open as Web App (if you see it), then tap Add. Without it, the icon opens the site in Safari and the app’s notifications never arrive.

Frequently asked questions

From the concept to everyday use — what parents ask most before getting started.

How does child custody work after a divorce?
Every family lands on its own arrangement — formal joint custody, sole custody with visitation, or an agreement that was never taken to court. In all of them the practical challenge is the same: knowing who has the child on each day, how holidays and vacations work, and what to do when one of you needs to swap a day. That everyday agreement is what the app organizes — whatever shape the custody takes.
What is a parenting calendar?
It's the agreement that defines whose day each day is — alternating weeks, fixed days, rotating weekends, holidays. When it lives only in each parent's head, or scattered across a chat, it becomes a source of friction. Entrelares puts that calendar in one place, identical for both parents, with every day swap approved by the other parent and recorded with date and time — less arguing about what was agreed, and more predictability for the children.
Is the app available in English?
Yes. The whole app interface is available in English and Portuguese, and you can switch language at any time in your profile. What is never translated is what you write yourself — names, notes and messages stay exactly as you typed them. Prices are charged in Brazilian reais (see Pricing).
Does the other parent need an account too?
The app works best with both of you: you create the family and send an invitation by e-mail. It's with two accounts that day swaps gain mutual approval — the heart of the tool. You can start on your own and invite the other parent whenever you want.
Can the history be used as evidence in court?
The app permanently records every swap, approval and change, with date and time, in a history that cannot be edited or deleted. The period's PDF report (Premium) carries a QR code with which whoever receives it checks that the file was not altered. That record can be useful to document how the parenting agreement was followed — but whether any document counts as evidence is always the judge's decision, and the rules differ from country to country. When in doubt, talk to your lawyer.
Are my data and my children's data safe?
Yes. Each family sees only its own data — that separation is enforced in the database, not just on screen. We show no ads and sell no data, and we follow the LGPD, Brazil's data protection law. See our Privacy Policy (published in Portuguese).
How much does the app cost?
The essentials are free: the calendar for both parents, swaps approved by both, notifications and the history. Premium costs R$ 5,49/month (or R$ 54,90/year, with 2 months free) per family on the web version — a promotional launch price, valid until December 31, 2026 and on that channel only, a single amount that covers every caregiver; on Google Play, in the Android app, it costs R$ 5,99/month or R$ 59,90/year — and unlocks advanced features such as adding grandparents, a nanny and other caregivers beyond the couple, the child's full agenda, shared expenses, writing in the family chat, the PDF report verifiable by QR code and planning up to 24 months ahead. Payment is by Pix (a QR code in your banking app, no card details) or credit card, with a full refund within 7 days (article 49 of the Brazilian Consumer Code); cancel whenever you like without losing any data.
Can I also organise the children's agenda and expenses?
Yes. The child's agenda gathers school, health, medicines and activities for each child, with routines that repeat and reminders; the day note is free, and the rest of the agenda is part of Premium. Shared expenses (Premium) split each expense equally, by amount, by percentage or by shares, show each person's balance and the fewest payments to settle up. A payment counts only once the person receiving it confirms, and the app never moves money.
Can grandparents or a nanny just follow along, without changing anything?
Yes, as a viewer: they see the calendar, the agenda and the reports and get the informative notifications in the app, but they change nothing, take no part in swaps and see neither the swap messages nor the expenses. The free plan already includes a viewer; Premium allows more.
Is there a conversation between the caregivers inside the app?
Yes. The family chat is a single one, for everyone, and no message can be edited or deleted; each one shows who has read it. Writing is part of Premium; without it, what was already said stays available to read. Whoever generates the PDF report can include the conversation for the period.
Does it work on iPhone and Android?
Yes. The app runs on any phone with a browser — Android or iPhone — and can be installed on the home screen in seconds, and on Android from Google Play too. See the installation steps.
What if the other parent doesn't approve a swap?
The swap simply doesn't happen: the day stays exactly as the calendar has it, and the refusal is recorded in the history. If they don't answer, the request is approved automatically once the deadline passes, and the history records that it was for lack of an answer. A day changes hands only through the request and the answer — and when an administrator corrects a day alone, the history says that too. That's what keeps the calendar trustworthy for both of them.
